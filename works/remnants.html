Solo

Remnants

of time and space
Program Note

“Every place leaves its mark upon those who live in or visit that place, just as travelers and residents leave remnants of their own existence behind.” This philosophy forms the core of Joseph Dangerfield’s Remnants, a highly evocative solo piano suite that acts as a psychological and geographical travelogue. Composed during a period when Dangerfield lived in Russia, the work directly channels the contrasting spirits of two specific locations: the bustling, historically layered capital of Moscow and the vast, deeply silent expanse of Lake Baikal in Siberia.

The piece explores the intersection of physical geography and human memory through a continuous, seven-movement arch. Part I focuses intently on the concepts of time, movement, and physical space. Its initial movements construct a fluid continuum that is abruptly shattered in the third movement, where a striking descending gesture from the previous movement is re-contextualized to interrupt the established flow. Part II shifts focus toward the natural world, meditating on the fluctuations, vastness, and atmospheric changes of the Siberian wilderness. Musically, Dangerfield uses the full sonic and expressive range of the piano—from aggressive, rhythmically driving gestures to fragile, ringing resonance—to capture the physical and emotional “remnants” left behind by time and travel.

— Joseph Dangerfield
