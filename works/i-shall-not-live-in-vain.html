I Shall Not Live in Vain

for SATB chorus
Program Note

Much like its companion piece I Wish, I Shall Not Live in Vain looks past the weight of profound mourning to seek comfort in our small, everyday responsibilities toward one another.

The piece sets the famous, deeply moving poem of the same name by Emily Dickinson (1830–1886). Dickinson’s text rejects grandiose declarations of legacy. Instead, it asserts that a life’s worth is validated through microscopic acts of compassion: easing a single heartbreak, cooling a localized pain, or assisting a “fainting robin” back to safety. It is a humble reflection of empathy, framing small acts of kindness as the ultimate defense against a meaningless existence.

Dangerfield’s musical adaptation captures the fragile, intimate essence of Dickinson’s verse. The setting favors a lyrical and deeply expressive texture where the vocal lines overlap and unfold with a sense of natural breath. Rather than utilizing static, traditional choral phrasing, the harmony shifts with a delicate vulnerability—effortlessly navigating the space between internal longing and radiant hope. The piece leaves audiences with a lingering, resonant reminder of our shared duty to comfort and heal.
