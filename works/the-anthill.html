The Anthill

for narrator, soprano, baritone, and piano
Composer's Note

The Anthill was composed in 2011 during an artist residency at the historic Yaddo Artist Colony. A deep exploration of nature, history, and philosophy, the expansive 45-minute dramatic chamber work underwent a thorough revision by the composer in the summer of 2026.

The piece functions as a theatrical and musical setting of nine selected poems extracted from the celebrated long-poem The Anthill by his close collaborator and friend, F.D. Reeve. Reeve’s text centers on the life, mind, and transcendental philosophy of Henry David Thoreau. Dangerfield utilizes the unique vocal combination of a narrator alongside soprano and baritone soloists to flesh out the multifaceted layers of Thoreau’s world—ranging from stark biographical reflections to abstract philosophical musings.

Musically, the work is organized into flexible subsets and can be performed as a complete immersive cycle or as independent standalone acts. Across its movements, Dangerfield’s writing contrasts vivid, rhythmically driving numbers with deeply introspective, coloristic textures.

Throughout the cycle, the piano acts as far more than mere accompaniment; it serves as the landscape. Its complex registers mirror the rustlings of the woods, the frantic movements of the natural ecosystem, and the vast psychological depths of an iconic American thinker. The Anthill stands as a monumental fusion of structural contemporary classical music, literature, and historical biography.
